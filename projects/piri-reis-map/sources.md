# Source chain

1. **The map (1513):** Piri Reis (Piri ibn Hacı Mehmed), nephew of Kemal Reis, drew it at Gallipoli in Muharrem 919
   AH (9 March to 7 April 1513), per his own note on the map. Gazelle-skin parchment; only the western part survives.
   Topkapı Palace Library, Istanbul.
2. **Rediscovery (1929):** Gustav Adolf Deissmann found it while cataloguing the Topkapı library.
3. **The notes:** translated by Yusuf Akçura (Türk Tarih Kurumu, 1935). They include Saint Brendan's monks landing on
   a fish; "oxen with one horn, and also monsters"; "These monsters are seven spans long... But they are harmless
   souls"; that the map was compiled from about twenty charts and mappae mundi, "and also from a map drawn by
   Colombo in the western region"; and that a Spaniard held by Kemal Reis "had been three times to that land with
   Colombo".
4. **The Antarctica claim:** Charles Hapgood, *Maps of the Ancient Sea Kings* (1966); Erich von Däniken,
   *Chariots of the Gods?* (1968).
5. **Rebuttal:** Gregory C. McIntosh, *The Piri Reis Map of 1513* (University of Georgia Press, 2000): the southern
   coast is South America, turned east to fit the parchment; the map is the best surviving witness to Columbus's
   lost chart.
6. **Antarctica:** continental ice sheet since about 34 million years ago; first sighted in January 1820
   (Bellingshausen and Lazarev; Bransfield).

Caution in the narration: "Most historians read that coast as South America" (not "proved"); "may be its last echo".
"His uncle had captured a sailor": the note says the late Gazi Kemal had a Spanish slave who had sailed with Colombo.
