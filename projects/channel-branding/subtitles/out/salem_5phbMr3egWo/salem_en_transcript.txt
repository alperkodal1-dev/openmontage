If you lived in Salem in 1692, someone else's nightmare could get you killed.
It began in winter. Two little girls began to scream.
They twisted. They crawled beneath the chairs.
They swore invisible hands were pinching them in the dark.
An enslaved woman named Tituba confessed.
A tall man in black showed her a book, and she made her mark in it.
Soon, 50 more confessed.
Strangers searched their bodies for the devil's mark,
and the witches of Salem burned. Or so the story goes.
But the records say otherwise. Not one witch was burned in Salem.
Under English law, witchcraft was a felony, and the punishment was the rope.
19 people were hanged.
One man, Giles Corey, was crushed beneath stones for refusing to plead.
The main proof: dreams and visions.
In 1697, a judge stood in church and begged forgiveness.
The last name was cleared in 2022.
So, was there ever magic in Salem?
Or was fear the only spell that ever worked? Real or myth?
