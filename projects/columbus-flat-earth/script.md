## Spoken text (for the voiceover)

Columbus never proved the Earth was round. In 1492, every educated European already knew. And the experts who told him no? They were right. Around 240 BC, Eratosthenes measured the planet using shadows in two Egyptian cities. He was close. Medieval scholars kept that alive. Bede called the Earth a globe. Sacrobosco's book, The Sphere, was taught in universities for centuries. So what did Spain's scholars argue with Columbus about? Size. Columbus shrank the globe by about a quarter. The experts said Asia was too far to reach by sailing west. Without America in the way, his crews would have starved. So where did the myth come from? In 1828, Washington Irving wrote a dramatic life of Columbus, with clergy quoting scripture against a round world. Schoolbooks repeated it. Even the famous "medieval" engraving of a man peering past the edge of the sky? First printed in Paris, in 1888. So who really believed the Earth was flat? Real or myth? Tell me below.
