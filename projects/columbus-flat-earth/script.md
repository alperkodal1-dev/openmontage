## Spoken text (for the voiceover)

Columbus never proved the Earth was round. In 1492, every educated European already knew. And the experts who told him no? They were right. Around 240 BC, Eratosthenes measured the planet with nothing but shadows in two Egyptian cities. He was close. Medieval monks taught it too. What sailors truly feared was the ocean itself. Old maps filled it with sea serpents and monsters. One globe even warns: here be dragons. Columbus's real mistake was size. He shrank the world by about a quarter. The experts said Asia was too far to reach. Without an unknown continent in the way, his crews would have starved. So who invented the flat Earth? In 1828, Washington Irving wrote a dramatic life of Columbus, with priests denying a round world. Schoolbooks repeated it. And that famous "medieval" engraving of a man peering past the edge of the sky? First printed in Paris, in 1888. So who really believed the Earth was flat: them, or us? Real or myth? Tell me below.
